# World Atlas: content and scientific interpretation

Updated 2026-09-08. This document covers the World Atlas graph in `data.ts`; the existing molecular laboratory has its separate calibration notes in `SCIENCE.md`. The registry has 8,238 context-preserving addresses, six named families, nine trails, ten quizzes and four mechanisms. Repeated molecular/cellular lessons retain separate ancestry under each material or tissue; this is not a claim of 8,238 distinct scientific concepts.

The content is original English/French educational prose. Every node has source links, an explicit interpretation of its size, a question and an answer. It is not a claim of curriculum certification, endorsement by an education ministry, diagnostic usefulness, or an exhaustive anatomical or molecular reconstruction.

## What a level means

The graph distinguishes structures found **inside** something (`contains`), chemical or structural **constituents** (`madeOf`), and a **selected sample** or region (`sample`). It is a navigation tree, not a complete ontology or inventory. Repeated carbon or oxygen branches have separate IDs so returning outward restores the biological/material context that was actually entered.

`sizeMeters` is the largest characteristic extent of the depicted object or selected segment. The note beside it is part of the measurement: a 120 µm muscle-fiber segment does not mean that an entire muscle fiber is only 120 µm long. Some numbers are chosen windows (for example, a 10 nm DNA excerpt), not measurements claimed by a paper. Dimensions of variable biological examples are approximate nominal values, not universal constants. Root `world` is a composite scene and has no single physical diameter.

The landscape combines nearby objects with a distant cloud. A cloud hundreds of meters across must not be presented as physically the size of a person merely because their on-screen extents are similar.

## Anatomical surfaces, provenance and scale

The macroscopic body uses [BodyParts3D 4.0](https://dbarchive.biosciencedbc.jp/en/bodyparts3d/download.html), supplemented by lung surfaces from the official BodyParts3D 4.3 viewer. This is an anatomical reference database for an adult human male. The [official database README](https://dbarchive.biosciencedbc.jp/data/bodyparts3d/LATEST/README_e.html) describes the source and its compound-organ tables. Of Atlas's 14 GLBs, 13 use the 4.0 archive and `lungs.glb` uses the separate 4.3 supplement. All retain shared source spatial relationships while simplifying meshes and assigning explanatory colors. This is a reference reconstruction, not a population-average body, a diagnostic model or microscopic imaging. Blue vessel coloring does not mean blood is blue.

The source's skin spans 1719.4712 mm at its largest extent. Conversion applies one translation, rotation and uniform scale to all parts: source +X is patient left, +Y posterior and +Z superior; display +X is patient left, +Y superior and +Z anterior. Organ extents, source bounds and landmarks are recorded in the [manifest](../../public/models/bodyparts3d/manifest.json). Recentring an isolated organ does not change its physical scale or its location when returned to the body. Paired lungs and kidneys use the complete pair's extent. Context skeleton, muscle and vessel meshes exclude parts already present in the selectable structures.

The [asset README](../../public/models/bodyparts3d/README.md), [manifest](../../public/models/bodyparts3d/manifest.json) and [provenance](../../public/models/bodyparts3d/provenance.json) record source URLs, versions, licenses, hashes, part identities, transformations and simplification per asset. The 4.0 derivatives use the [current archive CC BY 4.0 terms](https://dbarchive.biosciencedbc.jp/en/bodyparts3d/lic.html), updated 27 February 2025. The separate 4.3 lung adaptation preserves **CC BY-SA 2.1 Japan**, as specified by the [official viewer information](https://lifesciencedb.jp/bp3d/info_en/index.html#License); the archive's newer license is not assumed to cover that distribution. Preserve both licenses and their attribution. Historical 4.0 OBJ headers with older wording remain documented separately.

An official compound-organ relation can describe an incomplete set of source surfaces. The 4.0 lung compounds supplied bronchial and vascular trees without the surrounding parenchyma. Atlas therefore uses 18 directly identified 4.3 segmental parenchyma meshes, **FJ6595–FJ6612**, for the lung surface. Their original coordinates remain aligned with the 4.0 body through the shared source transform; there is no independent lung resizing. The heart remains entirely 4.0 and includes **FJ2428 / FMA13884**, the ventricular wall identified in the source but omitted from the heart's PART-OF aggregate. Neither correction adds a guessed outer envelope. Source-part coverage and anatomical annotations remain partial; a source-derived surface does not establish every internal tissue layer.

Surface, organs/vessels, skeleton and muscle layers organize the macroscopic view. They do not change biological containment or create different bodies. Heart, lungs, brain, liver, paired kidneys, stomach, intestines and femur provide anatomical views; the selected muscle is the left biceps brachii, including its two heads. The selected vein is the whole left cephalic vein. Entering `human/vein/segment` selects a 4 cm explanatory vessel section, roughly 3 mm in diameter, before reaching blood. At that scale its lumen shows a continuous red volume; individual red cells would be too small for the oversized spheres of a schematic cell view. The subsequent blood sample supplies the cellular scale. The stable `human/vein/blood` address remains valid, with the new section in its ancestor path. Cells, alveoli, sarcomeres and molecular models reached from these surfaces are separate educational samples, not microscopic details extracted from the source meshes.

## Spatial samples and anatomical source identity

The spatial address records **where the learner chose to enter**, separately from **what the composition tree describes**. Each entry contains a real parent/child edge, the selected point in the parent's local frame, and one of three interpretations. A `sample` is a representative material/tissue window at that point; an `instance` is the actual selected visible constituent, anchored by its center; a `surface` is an approached point on the unchanged object's surface. Surface selection changes camera focus, never the location or scale of the whole anatomical organ or macroscopic object. Shared links, history and saved discoveries retain this address through `sites`, with finite-coordinate and graph validation.

A material window is not a tiny object previously suspended at a landmark inside its parent. `spatialOnly` records therefore retain composition edges and explicit physical sample sizes without creating a second visible object or a locator point. Material annotations must resolve to one appropriate direct child: a membrane, cytoplasmic region, liquid volume or source vessel cannot simply fall through to whichever child happens to be first. The six named world families remain distinct from additional ambient material samples.

The [compact anatomical sidecar](../../public/models/bodyparts3d/parts.json) identifies all **1,670 source parts** in the existing 14 GLBs. Its triangle ranges refer to the serialized merged mesh, with a SHA-256 match to the loaded asset. Names and FMA identities are copied from source OBJ headers; 11 parts have incomplete headers and retain null identity fields. Artery, vein, bone and muscle categories use the official IS-A class membership recorded by the converter. A left great saphenous vein, **FJ2103 / FMA21380**, is distinct from the selected left cephalic vein, **FJ2220 / FMA13326**. Both may lead to a representative venous-blood lesson, but the source part and chosen location remain different. An artery follows its own local arterial route. This identification uses the source anatomy, not its illustrative blue/red color.

Regional labels such as left/right arm or leg are **approximate navigation hints**, inferred from a part's name and broad source-coordinate zones. They are not FMA boundary assignments or precise tissue segmentations; a long vessel can cross more than one region. The full-body skin has no single region, so the selected surface point supplies its local hint. A restored URL must canonicalize anatomical identity instead of accepting a claimed category that conflicts with the source part.

No source vertex contains a measured microscopic tissue map. Opening blood, cortical bone, skeletal muscle, a cell's cytoplasm or a membrane at that point opens a labeled teaching sample. It does not claim a patient-specific tissue section, a unique molecular identity measured at that vertex, or a chemical cleavage caused by zooming. Fixed SI ratios and the sample-size note distinguish a selected viewing window from the complete organ. See the [official BodyParts3D model/representation documentation](https://lifesciencedb.jp/bp3d/info_en/index.html) for the distinction between anatomical contours and the concepts they represent, and the [asset documentation](../../public/models/bodyparts3d/README.md) for exact source and conversion records.

## Biological pathways and common traps

- **Mature human red cell → hemoglobin → heme → iron:** a mature human erythrocyte has expelled its nucleus and lacks mitochondria. Its branch therefore contains neither a cellular nucleus nor DNA. A lymphocyte supplies the separate blood-to-DNA route. The [OpenStax erythrocyte chapter](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-3-erythrocytes) documents its 7–8 µm diameter, biconcave shape and maturation. Platelets are a distinct case: they lack nuclei but retain organelles, including mitochondria, as discussed in [leukocytes and platelets](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/18-4-leukocytes-and-platelets).
- **Hemoglobin:** adult HbA has an α₂β₂ assembly and four hemes. The four-subunit identity and heme composition are grounded in [RCSB entry 4HHB](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/4HHB) and the [PDB-101 explanation](https://pdb101.rcsb.org/motm/41). The procedural fold is an explanatory model, not an imported experimental coordinate model. About 6 nm is a characteristic assembly extent, not the crystallographic unit-cell length. Fe²⁺ is the conventional functional heme-iron description; the actual coordinated electronic structure is not an isolated free ion.
- **White cell or living keratinocyte → cellular nucleus → chromatin → nucleosome → DNA → nucleotide:** the DNA helix has a sugar–phosphate backbone, with bases pairing between strands. A base pair is not the whole backbone. [NHGRI’s DNA fact sheet](https://www.genome.gov/about-genomics/fact-sheets/Deoxyribonucleic-Acid-Fact-Sheet) and [OpenStax nucleic acids](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/3-5-nucleic-acids) support this chemistry. The displayed DNA is a 10 nm-long segment of roughly 2 nm-wide B-DNA, not a 10 nm chromosome. The helix is schematic, without crystallographic groove detail.
- **Chromatin:** histones, DNA and other associated proteins make a dynamic structure. The app avoids the misleading claim that all interphase chromatin necessarily forms a regular 30 nm fiber. A nucleosome core is roughly 11 nm across and wraps about 147 base pairs around an eight-histone core; see [DNA packaging](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK534207/) and [nucleosome structural studies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3052702/). Selecting the DNA excerpt is an explanatory view of part of the structure, not chemically detaching the whole wrapped contour.
- **Lung → alveolus → adjacent capillary:** the capillary is on the blood side of the air–blood barrier, not inside the alveolar airspace. `sample` marks this adjacent-region transition. [Respiratory structures](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/22-1-organs-and-structures-of-the-respiratory-system) and [gas exchange](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/22-4-gas-exchange) support diffusion down partial-pressure differences. The 200 µm alveolus is representative and changes size with inflation.
- **Heart and skeletal muscle:** sarcomeres shorten through filament sliding; actin filaments do not themselves shrink. A skeletal muscle fiber is one long multinucleated cell. A cardiac muscle cell is usually centrally nucleated, often with one nucleus, sometimes two, and joins neighbors at intercalated discs. See [skeletal muscle](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-2-skeletal-muscle), [contraction](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-3-muscle-fiber-contraction-and-relaxation), and [cardiac muscle tissue](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-7-cardiac-muscle-tissue). The nominal cardiac/skeletal sarcomere lengths are 2.2/2.5 µm, not fixed lengths throughout a contraction cycle.
- **Skin and hair:** the living keratinocyte branch is explicitly from a deeper epidermal layer. It must not be confused with dead cornified surface cells or the keratinized hair shaft. Skin is not uniformly nucleated at every layer. Source: [skin layers](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/5-1-layers-of-the-skin) and [accessory structures](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/5-2-accessory-structures-of-the-skin).
- **Neurons:** the 100 µm sample is a local field, not a complete long axon. A chemical synapse has separated membranes; the action potential is a membrane-voltage phenomenon involving ion movement, not an electron traveling like one in a metal wire. Sources: [nervous tissue](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/12-2-nervous-tissue) and [neuron communication](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/12-5-communication-between-neurons).

Local liver, kidney, stomach and intestinal samples use explicitly selected cell examples, not a statement that every anatomical surface point has identical histology. A hepatocyte is not a keratinocyte, a renal tubular epithelial cell is not an entire nephron, and a cortical osteocyte is not a muscle cell. Their shared intracellular lessons describe general organelles without inheriting skin-specific questions. Compare [NIDDK's digestive-system overview](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/digestive-system-how-it-works) and [kidney function](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidneys-how-they-work).

Cytoplasmic and nucleoplasmic windows sample aqueous material and selected proteins. Cytoplasm includes the cytosol and cytoplasmic organelles; the cytosol is not synonymous with a whole cell. A nuclear-envelope patch depicts a local membrane, not the claim that the double envelope has become a single bilayer. Mitochondrial DNA is a selected helix segment, not a nuclear chromosome or complete mitochondrial genome. These distinctions follow [The Compartmentalization of Cells](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26907/). The correct chapter is NBK26907; NBK26882 concerns energy metabolism and must not be mislabeled as this source.

The generic protein view and selected globin fragment use illustrative folds and an unspecified sequence. A glycine residue teaches the peptide backbone with continuation bonds; it is not a free amino acid or an assigned glycine location in an experimental parent protein. A membrane contains both lipids and embedded proteins, with separate routes. See [The Shape and Structure of Proteins](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26830/).

The 300 µm cortical-bone field separates living cells, blood-containing canals and a mineralized matrix. Collagen and calcium-phosphate mineral are different components. The displayed Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆(OH)₂ motif counts an ideal hydroxyapatite composition; its positions and 1 nm teaching frame are not a measured crystal unit cell, a free molecule or the dimensions of a complete mineral platelet. Biological apatite contains substitutions and structural imperfections. The collagen view is only a short protein excerpt. See [Endotext's bone ultrastructure](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK279149/) and [Histology, Osteoblasts](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557792/).

## Wood, leaves and fungi

The wood model selects a flowering-tree vessel example; it does not imply that all tree species have vessels. Secondary xylem contains several cell types. Mature conducting elements are dead, while their walls remain useful. Cellulose reinforcement, hemicelluloses and lignin have distinct roles and should not be rendered as one homogeneous white substance. Source: [USDA Forest Products Laboratory, Wood Handbook (2021), chapter 3](https://www.fpl.fs.usda.gov/documnts/fplgtr/fplgtr282/fpl_gtr282.pdf), with [FPL nanostructure research](https://www.fpl.fs.usda.gov/documnts/pdf2016/fpl_2016_jakes001.pdf) for the few-nanometer elementary-fibril scale. Values depend on whether an author measures an elementary fibril or a larger bundle.

Cellulose is a polymer with β(1→4)-linked glucose-derived residues. A selected repeating residue has composition C₆H₁₀O₅; it must not be labeled as free glucose C₆H₁₂O₆. A chemical cleavage is different from changing magnification. The molecular model is a connectivity diagram, not an atom-complete, stereochemically validated cellulose coordinate model. [OpenStax carbohydrates](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/3-2-carbohydrates) supports the polymer/residue distinction and the related chitin chemistry. Lignin is a heterogeneous aromatic polymer, never a protein; its model uses the `polymer` kind rather than a protein fold.

The microfibril and chain layers have different meanings and models. `celluloseMicrofibril` depicts a 30 nm-long bundle only a few nanometers wide; `cellulose` depicts a single 8 nm chain excerpt with alternating residue orientations. Its ring outlines omit side groups, which the residue layer discusses separately. The bundle's illustrative 18-chain 2/3/4/4/3/2 arrangement follows a proposed model in [Molecular architecture of softwood revealed by solid-state NMR (2019)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-12979-9). It is not a universal measured count: [wood microfibril research (2023)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41477-023-01430-z) supports a 24-chain organization in its seed-plant samples. The chosen packing teaches aligned chains without claiming to settle species-dependent structure.

The mesophyll-cell route includes chloroplasts, a nucleus with a DNA branch, mitochondria and ribosomes. Plant nuclear chromosome numbers depend on species and ploidy; the shared nucleus description explicitly replaces the human chromosome-count fact. Plant mitochondria are expected: photosynthesis does not replace cellular respiration. A generic root or wood cell must not automatically receive chloroplasts. Photosynthesis supplies chemical energy from light while matter comes from CO₂ and water. Oxygen released during photosynthesis comes from water. Chlorophyll coordinates magnesium rather than heme’s iron. Thylakoid/leaf models are anatomical and biochemical diagrams, not complete photosystem structures. Sources: [leaves](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/30-4-leaves), [photosynthesis](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/8-1-overview-of-photosynthesis), and [light-dependent reactions](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/8-2-the-light-dependent-reactions-of-photosynthesis).

A root hair is an extension of a plant epidermal cell. A fungal hypha is a different biological structure. Fungi are not plants, do not photosynthesize, and commonly digest outside their cells before absorbing nutrients. Their walls commonly include chitin and glucans. The mushroom is a fruiting body, not necessarily the whole organism. Sources: [roots](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/30-3-roots) and [fungal characteristics](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/24-1-characteristics-of-fungi).

The selectable primary-wall matrices under leaf, root and grass cells are distinct from their cellulose fibers and from wood's lignin-containing secondary wall. Their non-cellulosic polysaccharides include pectins and hemicelluloses, in proportions that depend on plant group and cell type. [Wang et al. (2015)](https://academic.oup.com/plphys/article/168/3/871/6113743) directly measured cellulose–pectin proximity in never-dried Arabidopsis primary walls. That evidence supports polymer contacts; it does not establish universal proportions, a separately isolated matrix phase or measured coordinates for Atlas's grass/tree diagrams.

The fungal matrix is a separate sample of non-chitin polymers, including glucans and species-dependent mannose-containing components. [Chakraborty et al. (2021)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-26749-z), also available through [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8566572/), studies Aspergillus fumigatus and mutants with solid-state NMR. Its rigid/mobile polymer organization must not be presented as the measured wall of the unspecified mushroom in the scene. The 10 nm `polymer` sample is an explanatory network with representative C/O markers, not an atom-complete sequence, stoichiometric formula or experimental molecular geometry. Carbon and oxygen destinations use `worldSampleAtomicNumber` so an ambiguous generic atom annotation cannot choose an element arbitrarily.

## Water, clouds and minerals

- Water molecules contain two H atoms and one O. The isolated-molecule O–H distance of about 0.0958 nm and angle of about 104.5° are from [NIST CCCBDB](https://cccbdb.nist.gov/exp2x.asp?casno=7732185&charge=0). The 0.3 nm molecular extent is approximate because the chosen atomic-boundary convention affects it. A selected spherical region inside the liquid is a sampling boundary, not another droplet living inside water.
- A cloud contains dispersed liquid droplets and/or ice; the visible cloud is not gaseous water becoming white. [NOAA NESDIS clouds](https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/our-environment/clouds) supports this phase distinction. The 20 µm droplet is a representative point in a distribution, consistent with [NOAA AOML cloud-physics instrumentation](https://www.aoml.noaa.gov/hrd/HRD-P3_cloud.html), which measures cloud drops of approximately 5–47 µm with its small-drop instrument. It is not a universal cloud-drop size or a raindrop.
- A 100 µm ice crystal is an example, and a 2 nm lattice excerpt is a selected region. The sixfold schematic illustrates ordinary hexagonal ice, not a validated atomic unit cell. Many natural ice particles do not look like perfect six-branched flakes. [NOAA’s snowflake explanation](https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/about/k-12-education/ice-snow/how-do-snowflakes-form) discusses growth and symmetry. The old `scijinks.gov/snowflakes/` link was replaced with this current NOAA endpoint during review.
- [UCAR’s air composition](https://scied.ucar.edu/learning-zone/air-quality/whats-in-the-air) supports approximately 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen for **dry** air. Water-vapor abundance varies. N₂ and O₂ are molecules; an oxygen atom bonded in H₂O is not equivalent to dissolved O₂ available for respiration.
- A rock can contain several minerals; the route selects one 8 mm quartz grain. Its six prism faces, faceted termination and faint striations depict a macroscopic crystal habit; imperfections and colors are illustrative. See [USGS rocks versus minerals](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-difference-between-rock-and-mineral) and the [Mineralogical Society of America quartz handbook entry](https://www.handbookofmineralogy.org/pdfs/quartz.pdf).

The distinct atomic-network model uses 298 K α-quartz coordinates from [Sutter et al. (2022), tables 2–4](https://journals.iucr.org/j/issues/2022/04/00/te5094/), which tabulate Kihara's measurements. `mineralModels.ts` records a = 4.9137 Å, c = 5.4047 Å and the three-silicon/six-oxygen basis in the P3₂21 z(+) setting. A rotation into the display frame preserves handedness. Atom centers retain their common metric; colors, marker radii, bonds and translucent tetrahedral faces are explanatory.

The finite model contains 44 complete SiO₄ tetrahedra, with shared oxygens counted once. All silicon centers have four oxygen neighbors; interior oxygens connect two silicon centers with bent Si–O–Si links. Boundary oxygens have only one displayed silicon neighbor because the other lies outside the excerpt. Consequently, the clipped specimen's total atom counts do not themselves have to equal bulk SiO₂ stoichiometry. They must never be presented as a free molecule. The normalized visual extent, including the explicitly illustrative markers, is `1.7411510169506073e-9` m (about 1.74 nm); the same value in the registry preserves the coordinate-derived Si–O distances of approximately 1.605–1.613 Å. Changing the excerpt or marker geometry requires updating that calibration together with its size note.

The separate 1.8 nm glass-network view distorts a silicon–oxygen scaffold to explain connected material without repeated crystal positions. It is not an experimentally reconstructed amorphous configuration: its topology derives from the quartz scaffold, its bond lengths are not a measured glass distribution, and glass modifiers and defects are omitted. The historical [NBS silica-glass model paper](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/59/jresv59n2p139_A1b.pdf) supplies background about tetrahedral network models, not the coordinates of this renderer. Do not relabel this diagram as validated amorphous atomistic data. The soil view is likewise a selected mixture of mineral grains, organic material and water/air pores, with varying real composition; see [USGS soil](https://www.usgs.gov/educational-resources/whats-my-soil) and [organic carbon in soil](https://pubs.usgs.gov/sir/2017/5118/elements/C_Org/C_Org_txt.html).

## Atomic conventions and the endpoint of zoom

The graph explicitly selects isotopes H-1, C-12, N-14, O-16, Mg-24, Si-28, P-31, Ca-40 and Fe-56. These are examples, not an assertion that other isotopes are absent. `atomicNumber`, `massNumber` and formal charge are separate fields. A nucleus always has Z protons and A−Z neutrons; H-1 correctly has no neutron. Menu descendants sample nucleon types rather than claiming that an iron nucleus has only two nucleons.

Atomic extents are not sharply bounded physical spheres. The displayed C, O, H and Si values are twice the Royal Society of Chemistry’s listed covalent radii: C 0.15 nm, O 0.128 nm, H 0.064 nm, Si 0.228 nm. See the [RSC periodic table](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/) and the individual element links attached to each node. Fe²⁺ (~0.15 nm) and Mg²⁺ (~0.14 nm) are approximate illustrative ionic extents; coordination and spin conditions matter. The [University of Sheffield ionic-radius tables](https://winter.group.shef.ac.uk/webelements/iron/atom_sizes.html) make those dependencies explicit. Ionic numbers must not be described as measured diameters of a free neutral atom.

The added RSC covalent-radius conventions are [N](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/7/nitrogen) 0.71 Å, [P](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/15/phosphorus) 1.09 Å and [Ca](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/20/calcium) 1.74 Å: the corresponding doubled extents are 0.142, 0.218 and 0.348 nm. The Ca/P atomic views use neutral reference electron counts to teach element identity. They do not assert that bone contains isolated neutral calcium or phosphorus: calcium has ionic character in apatite, and phosphorus is bonded within phosphate. A phosphate group's formal charge is not a charge to assign independently to a displayed phosphorus atom. Size notes must preserve this distinction in both languages.

For A > 1, the nominal nuclear diameter is `2 × 1.2 fm × cbrt(A)`, the approximate nuclear-size model in [OpenStax properties of nuclei](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/10-1-properties-of-nuclei). It is a matter-size model, not identical to a measured charge radius. A selected proton uses about 1.68 fm, twice the approximately 0.84 fm RMS charge radius documented by the [Particle Data Group](https://pdgprod.lbl.gov/pdgprod/pdgLive/DataBlock.action?node=S016CR). A neutron uses a similarly sized illustrative nucleon envelope, explicitly not a neutron charge-radius measurement.

Protons have uud and neutrons udd valence content, but gluons and quark–antiquark fluctuations are essential; a three-ball model is incomplete. Both quark flavors have separate selectable targets, with explicit `quarkFlavor` metadata: a clicked down marker must not open an up explanation. The stable `/quark` path selects a proton’s up or a neutron’s down representative; `/other-quark` selects the other flavor. One selected marker is a representative constituent, not a free extracted quark, and the two menu entries are types rather than a count of two physical quarks. Electrons are leptons, not quark composites. The [CERN Standard Model explanation](https://home.cern/science/physics/standard-model/) supports the elementary distinction. Quark and electron `sizeMeters` values are **null**: no assigned particle diameter, no invented next interior, no claim of a literal colored surface. The elementary limit is a scientific boundary, not a suggestion that the application hides another established layer.

## Maintenance checks

Before adding a branch, verify its containment relationship and nominal-size meaning. Keep each question, answer, explanation, quiz option and mechanism step in both languages. Review source links close to the fact they support. The authoring audit checks positive finite non-null sizes, complete bilingual fields, valid ancestors and journeys, isotope counts, and the absence of a nuclear/DNA branch in mature red cells.

Source retrieval on the review date found several USGS/USDA endpoints returning HTTP 403 to automated clients while their indexed institutional content remained available; this is an access restriction, not evidence of source removal. Do not silently replace those sources with unsourced summaries. Procedural biological diagrams do not import textbook illustrations or experimental protein folds. BodyParts3D supplies explicitly attributed anatomical surfaces, and quartz atom centers use cited crystallographic data; those sources must remain distinguishable from schematic geometry.


`scripts/verify-anatomy.mjs` defines bilingual browser checks for source meshes, layer-specific picks, source scale and anchors, return paths, locale/renderer continuity, phone access and download recovery. Its `assertAnatomyParts(locale)` guard reads every GLB's serialized buffers and validates face/vertex ownership, source identities, physical bounds, hashes, licenses and explicit uncertainty in region/unnamed-part metadata. `verify-spatial-world.mjs` checks source-preserving local sampling and address restoration. `scripts/mineral-models.mjs` defines the same geometry/content assertions in English and French, including calibrated quartz bond lengths, coordination, element identities, distinct cellulose levels and disposal. These maintenance checks state what must be tested, not the validation status of any deployment.

`node scripts/spatial-models.mjs` checks both locales without a browser and is also called through `assertSpatialModels(locale)` in `verify-spatial-world.mjs`. It verifies material annotations against actual direct children, ray hits on wall matrix surfaces, distinct fiber destinations, explicit C/O routing, root-cell contents and model normalization/disposal. The anatomy guard pins the reviewed provenance and aggregate geometry fingerprints in addition to validating their mutual consistency. New source geometry or a provenance correction requires a documented review and explicit fingerprint update; selection metadata alone must preserve them.
